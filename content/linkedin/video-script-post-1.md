# Video Script — "Buying a dental chair has never been easier"

**Source post:** Post 1, `2026-08-posts-dental-life.md`
**Target:** 60-75 seconds, 1:1 or 4:5 for LinkedIn feed, hard-subtitled
**Format:** Talking head (Vikas) + cutaway b-roll
**Voice:** Vikas, first person, unhurried. This is not an ad read.

---

## Why this post first

Post 1 is the right pilot for video because the idea is visual (a machine, a payment schedule, an empty operatory), the argument is linear, and the payoff lands in one sentence. Post 2 is too abstract for 60 seconds and Post 3 is better as a carousel.

---

## Script

**[0:00-0:06] HOOK — talking head, tight**

> Buying a dental chair has never been easier.
> Zero down payment. Zero percent EMI. Delivered in a week.

*On screen text: "0% EMI. Delivered in a week."*

**[0:06-0:16] TENSION — talking head, pull back slightly**

> And that's the problem.
> Your chairside hours are limited. The list of things you can buy for your clinic is endless.
> Often it isn't even the machine you want. It's the feeling of finally having a complete setup.

*B-roll: slow push in on a new, still-wrapped unit in an otherwise empty operatory.*

**[0:16-0:26] THE LINE — back to talking head**

> We are taught how to treat.
> Nobody teaches us how to buy.

*Hold on face. One beat of silence after this line — do not cut early.*

**[0:26-0:40] THE COST — b-roll over VO**

> When a CBCT becomes an EMI, you're committing three years of future collections before a single patient has walked in.
> The excitement of the new machine fades in about a month.
> The instalment stays for years.

*B-roll: a calendar, instalment rows ticking forward. Keep it plain, not slick.*

**[0:40-0:55] THE DISTINCTION — talking head**

> Borrowing to invest in your skill almost always pays back. A good hands-on course. Better magnification. Six months assisting someone genuinely better than you.
> Borrowing for equipment your case-mix doesn't justify yet locks you in. You can't say no to any case. You can't take a break. You can't wait for the right associate.

**[0:55-1:05] CLOSE — talking head, direct to camera**

> Buy the machine when your cases demand it. Not when the EMI makes it feel affordable.
> Sochiye — if you removed every machine from your clinic tomorrow, how much of your income would still walk in the door for you?

*End card: the Post 1 hot-take image, 2 seconds. No logo sting, no music swell.*

---

## Production notes

**Do not** use stock footage of smiling models in scrubs. It reads as an ad instantly and this piece only works if it reads as Vikas thinking out loud. If b-roll can't be shot in a real clinic, generated b-roll is better than stock — but it must be plain and unglamorous.

**Captions are mandatory.** Most of the feed watches muted. Burn them in rather than relying on LinkedIn's auto-captions, and keep them to 3-4 words per line.

**Pacing:** the silence after "Nobody teaches us how to buy" is the most important 1.5 seconds in the video. Every automated pipeline will want to cut it. Don't let it.

**Length discipline:** if it runs past 80 seconds, cut the b-roll section at 0:26-0:40 and let the line carry it.

---

## Hinglish and TTS

"Sochiye" is the one Hindi word in the script and it is load-bearing — it's what makes the closing question feel like Vikas rather than a script. Synthetic voices frequently mangle Devanagari-origin words inside an English sentence.

Test this word specifically before committing to any TTS vendor. If it can't say "sochiye" naturally, either record the closing line in Vikas's real voice and splice it, or drop the word rather than shipping a mispronunciation — the audience will notice, and a wrong Hindi pronunciation from an Indian founder is worse than no Hindi at all.
